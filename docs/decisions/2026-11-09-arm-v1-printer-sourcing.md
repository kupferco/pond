# Decision — Arm V1, printer and UK vs Brazil sourcing (9 Nov 2026)

## Arm V1: absorb the cost

- For the first MVP, buy six Feetech STS3215 servos (12 V, 30 kg·cm) at retail and pay the import tax. About $16 each retail; about $10.60 each at 1,000+ on Alibaba.
- No more time spent on ready-made SO-101 kit versus parts for V1. The goal is a working arm, not the cheapest arm.
- Keep the servo mounts standard, so cheaper servos can drop in later.
- Cost-cutting (home-built servos, assembly in Brazil) is an R&D track for V2/V3.

## Printer

- Plain Bambu Lab A1 (256 mm bed, no AMS), bought in Brazil (~R$3,191 vs ~£215 in the UK).
- Pending a chat with Dani's mother.

## UK vs Brazil sourcing

- Brazilian import tax can roughly double the price of electronics, so small, valuable electronics come from the UK in the cabin bag: the six servos, the servo driver board, a USB cable and a 12 V power supply with a 100–240 V input (Brazil uses 127 V and 220 V).
- Bulky, generic parts are bought in Brazil: pipe, tank, plywood, filament, N20 motors, and the printer.
- Duty-free allowance is US$1,000 by air; keep receipts and use things before flying.
